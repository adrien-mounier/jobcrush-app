#157 · Design B — “we changed what prints”

The question: the machine changed what prints. How does she see that, and put it back for this one application, in one control rather than four?

Five callers, one row frame. A country page left off her date of birth · the tailor printed 10 of 13 lines for one role · the sanity gate withheld a block it read as a mistake · a French line was translated · a certification expired. Two verbs — left off (put it back) and changed (is this still yours?) — so the row frame generalises and the control on the row does not. That is Design A’s own precedent.

Reusing what ships: the dark register, the CV paper, the option buttons, the tl-settle landing beat and the ending block are /tailor’s, at their own values. Nothing here invents a palette or a timing.

🚨 The paper cannot show four of the six changes. The shipped miniature renders summary, experience, skills, education — no letterhead, no certifications, no additional, which is exactly where a date of birth, a driving licence and an expired certification live. A put-back today would land somewhere she cannot see. That cost is the same for all three variants; the paper below shows the parts that would have to be added.
