## 1. Recommended Section Order

> **Superseded for this project (2026-06-11):** section order is fixed by the root CV -
> Professional Experience comes BEFORE Skills (council-validated 4-1, see
> council-transcript-20260611_211126.md). Do NOT apply item 3 below (Core Competencies before
> Experience). Project order: Summary, Professional Experience, Personal Projects, Skills,
> Education, Additional Information.

The 2026 standard for a senior IT PM CV follows a "strategic value first" sequence:

1. **Header** — Name, LinkedIn URL, location (city + remote/hybrid availability). Phone and email are mandatory; keep them in the body, not in a header or footer (ATS strips those).
2. **Professional Summary** — 3–4 lines; your elevator pitch.
3. **Core Competencies / Key Skills** — Categorized grid; scannable by recruiters and ATS alike.
4. **Professional Experience** — Reverse chronological; focus on the last 10–15 years.
5. **Certifications** — PMP, SAFe, cloud-specific certs. High-value credentials get dedicated visibility.
6. **Education** — Typically after certifications for senior profiles.
7. **Technical Environment (optional)** — Concise list of tech stacks managed (tools, platforms, ecosystems).

### Notes on ordering flexibility

- If certifications are a hard requirement in the offer (e.g., PMP required), move certifications above experience to ensure immediate visibility.
- A "Technical Environment" or "Digital Ecosystem" block can be merged into the Skills section rather than standing alone.
- Avoid creative section names ("My Career Path", "Where I've Worked"). Standard labels aid ATS categorization.

## 2. Ideal CV Length

- **Strictly 2 pages** is the 2026 standard, even for 20+ years of experience.
- The dominant philosophy is "greatest hits": prioritize recent, high-impact roles. Condense or drop experience older than 15 years.
- Use a **single-column layout**. Two-column layouts cause ATS parsing failures — the parser reads left-to-right and merges the two columns into unintelligible text.
- Avoid tables, text boxes, graphics, sidebars: these are invisible to many parsers (especially Workday-based systems).

> ✅ **What survives from below:** *quantified value* (accomplishment beats capability claim) and the

### Structure

Use a **3-part narrative** — not an "objective" statement. Objectives focus on what you want; summaries focus on what you deliver.

| Part | What it covers | Example |
|------|---------------|---------|
| **1. Identity** | Title + years of experience | "Senior IT Project Manager with 9+ years of experience..." |
| **2. Specialisation** | Domain focus or methodology strength | "...specialising in large-scale cloud migrations and AI-integrated delivery..." |
| **3. Quantified value** | A signature outcome or differentiator | "...consistently delivering on €5M+ portfolios with measurable ROI gains and zero critical deployment failures." |

### Guidelines

- Length: 3–4 lines, 60–80 words maximum.
- No first-person pronouns.
- No generic filler ("results-driven", "dynamic", "passionate").
- Tailor the specialisation phrase per offer — this is the highest-leverage customisation point.
- Include 1–2 high-signal keywords from the job description (methodology name, domain) to aid ATS.

## 4. Bullet Structure for Experience Sections

### The formula

**[Strong action verb] + [scope / context] + [quantified outcome or measurable impact]**

- Poor (task-based): "Responsible for managing project timelines."
- 2026 standard: "Reduced time-to-market by 15% through automated risk-tracking across 3 concurrent delivery streams."

### Action verbs — recommended for IT PM

Led, Delivered, Orchestrated, Coordinated, Prioritised, Owned, Defined, Scaled, Automated, Aligned, Translated, Facilitated, Negotiated, Mitigated, Governed, Streamlined, Accelerated, Secured, Established.

### Density and length

- One to two lines per bullet. Three lines is the absolute maximum.
- Aim for 4–6 bullets per role; prioritise quality over completeness.
- Use figures (€2M, 12 stakeholders, 30%) — they scan faster and carry more weight than spelled-out numbers.
- No "wall of text" bullets. If a bullet exceeds two lines, split or trim it.

### AI-specific framing (2026 trend)

Hiring managers specifically look for AI integration within delivery work. Where truthful, show:
- How AI tools were used to improve forecasting, reporting, or documentation.
- The efficiency gain or outcome, not just the tool name.
- Example: "Leveraged AI-driven predictive analytics to improve sprint forecasting accuracy by 20% across 5 squads."

### What to avoid

- Bullets that describe attendance or routine tasks ("Attended daily standups", "Prepared weekly reports").
- Vague outcomes ("Successful deployment", "Improved efficiency").
- Passive voice or noun-heavy phrasing ("Was responsible for the management of...").

## 5. Skills Section

### The "T-Shaped" model

- **Horizontal bar (breadth):** Broad technical literacy and cross-domain leadership.
- **Vertical stem (depth):** Deep expertise in one or two delivery models (e.g., SAFe, DevSecOps).
- Aim for a **70/30 split**: 70% technical/domain skills, 30% leadership/governance skills.
- Do not label a section "Soft Skills" — it signals filler. Use outcome-oriented category names instead.

### Recommended categories (2026)

| Category label | What goes here |
|----------------|---------------|
| **Methodologies & Frameworks** | Scrum, SAFe, Kanban, Hybrid, Prince2, DevOps, Lean |
| **Project Governance** | RAID management, change management, steering committees, vendor management, budget governance |
| **AI & Automation** | Predictive scheduling tools, LLM-based documentation, workflow automation (Power Automate, etc.) |
| **Digital Ecosystems & Tooling** | Jira (+ Advanced Roadmaps), Confluence, Azure DevOps, MS Project, Power BI, Notion |
| **Cloud & Infrastructure Literacy** | AWS/Azure/GCP awareness, FinOps, DevSecOps concepts |
| **Stakeholder & Leadership** | Executive stakeholder management, cross-functional alignment, distributed team leadership, change adoption |

### On skill ratings (stars, bars, percentages)

**Do not use them.** The consensus in 2026:
- ATS systems often cannot read graphical ratings; the skill may be silently dropped.
- Star ratings are subjectively meaningless to recruiters without a benchmark.
- Rating yourself 5/5 invites intense scrutiny; 3/5 can trigger automatic "low proficiency" filters.

**Alternative:** Use contextual proficiency labels in plain text: *Expert*, *Advanced*, *Proficient*, *Familiar*. Or better yet, demonstrate depth via bullet context in the experience section.

Example:
- Weak: Jira (★★★★☆)
- Better: Jira Service Management — Advanced (workflow automation, JQL reporting, portfolio management)

## 6. ATS Optimization

### Keyword strategy

- **Exact mirror:** If the JD says "Stakeholder Engagement," use that exact phrase — not "Client Relations" or "Partner Management."
- **Full name + abbreviation on first use:** "Project Management Professional (PMP)", "Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe)."
- **Contextual embedding:** Do not list keywords in isolation. Embed them inside achievement bullets. Semantic AI parsers (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever) now evaluate how skills connect to outcomes, not just their presence.
- **No keyword stuffing:** A skills cloud at the bottom of the CV is an outdated pattern and triggers spam filters in modern ATS.

### High-priority keyword clusters for IT PM (2025–2026)

| Cluster | Keywords |
|---------|----------|
| **Methodologies** | Hybrid Project Management, SAFe 6.0, Scrum, Kanban, Lean Six Sigma, Value Stream Mapping |
| **Emerging tech** | Generative AI integration, AI-powered project controls, digital transformation, LLM governance |
| **Governance / Risk** | Risk management, RAID log, cybersecurity risk, GDPR compliance, change management, ADKAR |
| **Strategy** | OKRs, ROI analysis, roadmap, portfolio management, business case |
| **Tooling** | Jira, Confluence, Azure DevOps, Power BI, MS Project, Jira Advanced Roadmaps |

### Formatting rules for ATS compatibility

| Rule | Correct | Avoid |
|------|---------|-------|
| Layout | Single-column, plain left-aligned | Two columns, sidebars |
| Containers | Paragraph / bullet text | Tables, text boxes |
| Icons / graphics | None | Phone icons, star ratings, skill bars |
| Contact info placement | Body of the document | Header / footer (many parsers strip these) |
| Separators | Dash, comma | Pipe character `|` (can cause word merging) |
| Dates | Month YYYY or MM/YYYY | Two-digit year ('26 breaks experience calculators) |
| Section headings | "Work Experience", "Skills", "Certifications" | Creative titles ("My Journey", "Tech Stack") |

### File format

- **DOCX** is the safest and most universally compatible format across all ATS systems.
- **PDF** is acceptable if the PDF is text-based (text can be highlighted/selected). Never use scanned or image-flattened PDFs.
- The "plain text test": save the CV as `.txt` and verify that sections are in order, words are not merged, and contact info is present. If the `.txt` is scrambled, the ATS view will be too.

## 7. What to Omit or Avoid

### Outdated content

- **"References available upon request"** — assumed; wastes space.
- **Career Objective statements** — replaced by the Professional Summary.
- **Generic soft skills lists** — ("team player", "hardworking", "good communicator"). These are noise. Demonstrate these traits through specific bullets instead.
- **"MS Office" in tools** — assumed for any professional; omit unless the JD specifically asks for advanced Excel or PowerPoint skills.
- **Outdated tools** — only include legacy tools if the specific JD asks for them.
- **Photo, age, marital status, nationality** — not standard in most EU and UK markets; legally sensitive and unnecessary unless visa status is directly relevant.
- **Hobbies and interests** — omit for senior roles unless the role context makes them directly relevant.

### Outdated patterns

- **Responsibility-based bullets** — describing daily tasks rather than outcomes is the most common red flag for senior PM profiles.
- **Two-column or heavily designed layouts** — visually impressive to a human but frequently catastrophic in ATS.
- **Skill ratings (stars, percentages, bars)** — see §5.
- **Overly long summaries** — more than 5 lines reads as unfocused and wastes prime real estate.
- **Too much technical depth** — listing Python syntax or SQL query types is appropriate for a developer CV, not a senior PM CV. The PM's role is project success; technology is the "how," not the headline.

### Red flags for recruiters

- Bullets that start with "Responsible for..." or "Was involved in..."
- Vague results: "improved performance", "achieved goals", "successful delivery" with no metric.
- Gaps in experience with no explanation.
- Experience sections that go back 20+ years with full bullet coverage — compress distant history.
- Mismatched dates or contradictions across roles.
- Describing yourself as a "Scrum purist" — signals inflexibility; hybrid is the 2026 norm.

## 8. 2026-Specific Trends

### AI integration in delivery

- AI experience is now **expected** for senior IT PMs, not a differentiator.
- Hiring managers are wary of "AI-washing." Show **operationalised AI**: specific tools used in the delivery lifecycle, with measurable outcomes.
- High-value signals: predictive risk management, AI-assisted reporting/documentation, workflow automation, agentic tooling.
- Mention responsible AI: data governance, hallucination risk awareness, AI ethics in a project context.
- Candidate who only "used AI for emails" is a red flag; candidate who automated 40% of status reporting or improved forecasting accuracy by 20% is a strong signal.

### Hybrid delivery as the default

- "Hybrid" delivery (combining Waterfall governance with Agile execution) is the dominant real-world model in 2026. Presenting as a "pure Scrum" or "pure Waterfall" practitioner can read as dated.
- Framing to use: "structured governance with agile execution", "adaptive delivery", "hybrid project orchestration."
- New 2026 KPIs to reference where truthful: Deployment Frequency, Mean Time to Recovery (MTTR), Value Realization Rate — alongside traditional cost/schedule metrics.

### Power skills as differentiators

With AI removing administrative overhead, hiring managers now weight "human-centric" leadership more heavily:
- **Strategic influence**: using data to shape decisions and push back with evidence.
- **Business translation**: converting complex technical programmes into ROI narratives for executives.
- **Emotional intelligence and change adoption**: leading teams through AI-induced organisational change.
- **Decision-making under uncertainty**: human judgment where statistical signals are ambiguous.

### Certifications in demand

- PMP remains the baseline for senior IT PM credibility.
- SAFe 6.0 is increasingly expected where agile at scale is relevant.
- Cloud literacy certifications (AWS, Azure — even Practitioner level) signal relevant technical awareness.
- AI-related credentials are emerging (PMI AI+ certification, Google AI, MIT digital transformation) — not yet mandatory but differentiating.
- "Hybrid stack" is the norm: a core PM cert + at least one methodology or domain credential.

### Framing shift: 2024 vs. 2026

| Dimension | 2024 (outdated) | 2026 (current) |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| Methodology | "Expert in Agile/Scrum" | "Hybrid delivery orchestrator" |
| AI experience | "Used Generative AI for emails" | "AI-driven predictive risk and reporting" |
| Success metric | "On time, on budget" | "ROI, adoption, business value realised" |
| Leadership framing | Command and control | Adaptive, cross-functional, people-first |
| Tools language | List tools in isolation | Embed tools in outcome-oriented bullets |

## Summary — Key Principles to Apply

1. Two pages. Single column. Standard headings. PDF or DOCX.
2. Summary: 3-part (identity + specialisation + quantified value), 60–80 words.
3. Bullets: action verb + scope + measurable outcome. One to two lines each. No task descriptions.
4. Skills: categorised by domain, no ratings, proficiency shown through context.
5. ATS: mirror JD terminology exactly; embed keywords in bullets, not keyword clouds.
6. AI: show operationalised AI with outcomes; "AI-washing" is a red flag.
7. Hybrid delivery: position as default mode, not exception.
8. Remove: ratings, two-column layout, generic soft skills, "responsible for" bullets, experience beyond 15 years in detail.
