**Date compiled:** 2026-07-10

In 2026, the "AI resume paradox" defines the hiring landscape: while 82% of companies use AI to screen
resumes, 49% of hiring managers automatically dismiss applications they identify as machine-generated
[1, 2]. Detection is intuitive and rapid — over 33% of managers spot AI tells within 20 seconds [3, 4].
AI-generated text has a recognizable "fingerprint": a lack of rhythmic variance and reliance on
statistically overrepresented "safe" vocabulary [5-7].

## Banned Words

| Word | Why it's a tell | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| Delve | The single strongest vocabulary signal; 48x more common in AI than human text [8, 9] | Explore, examine, dig into [8, 10] |
| Tapestry | 35x more common in AI; signals generic "filler" metaphors [8, 11] | Mix, collection, combination [8] |
| Leverage | Statistically overrepresented buzzword used when specific actions are missing [12, 13] | Use, draw on, apply [10, 14] |
| Robust | AI defaults to this for "strong" due to high probability in training data [7, 15] | Solid, reliable, stable [16] |
| Seamless | 19x more common; used for vague "good vibes" without proof [8, 17] | Smooth, easy, effortless [16] |
| Multifaceted | 28x more common; suggests inability to name specific skills [8, 11] | Complex, layered, varied [8] |
| Furthermore | 15x more common; signals rigid, textbook-style transitions [18, 19] | Also, plus, in addition [18, 20] |
| Synergy | A "semantically bald" cliche that suggests outdated 2010-template usage [14, 21] | Collaboration, partnership [14] |
| Innovative | A self-applied "participation trophy" that carries zero information [14, 22] | (Show the specific result of the innovation) [14] |
| Pivotal | 16x more common; an abstract adjective that fails to quantify impact [8, 13] | Key, critical, essential [8] |

## Banned Openers ("Instant Deletes")

Hiring managers report extreme fatigue from repetitive AI-style introductions [23]. Avoid:

- "I hope this email finds you well" — the primary tell for machine-written outreach [24, 25]
- "It is with great enthusiasm that I submit my application..." — overly formal, flowery [26]
- "In today's fast-paced digital world..." — vague, grand sweeping opening, adds zero value [24, 27, 28]
- "I believe my multifaceted skills align perfectly..." — repetitive, generic structure [29]
- "I came across your impressive work/profile..." — a "fake compliment" that signals bot behavior [30, 31]

## Style Guidance: Humanizing Content

Bypassing detectors and human recruiters both come down to structural variance, not just synonym
swaps [32, 33]:

- **Vary sentence length (burstiness).** AI models generate uniform, repetitive sentence structures
  [34, 35]. Humans write in "bursts" — alternating short, punchy sentences with longer, complex ones
  [36, 37]. A mix of concise bullets and detailed examples signals authenticity [37, 38].
- **Increase "surprise" (perplexity).** AI chooses the most statistically likely words [34, 39]. Use
  specific, technically precise industry terminology and unique proper nouns (project names, team
  sizes, dollar amounts) to break the AI pattern [37, 39, 40].
- **Avoid the "summary closer."** AI tends to end paragraphs by restating the opener (e.g., "In
  summary, efficiency is key") [41]. End instead with a unique insight or result [42, 43].
- **Break the "rule of three."** AI models group items in threes constantly (e.g., "Fast, reliable, and
  affordable"). When every sentence follows this list-heavy rhythm, it loses impact — prioritize one
  single high-value outcome per bullet [44, 45].
- **Reject uniform tonal consistency.** Every section of a machine-written resume sounds equally
  polished [12, 46]. Authentic human writing varies naturally in tone and formality across different
  roles and achievement types [12, 47].
