In 2026, senior-level resumes must navigate a dual-evaluation landscape: structurally rigid enough for
ATS parsers to map data accurately, yet semantically rich enough for LLM-based screening layers and
human recruiters who average only 7.4 seconds per initial scan [1-3]. For senior professionals,
formatting is executive personal branding that signals strategic clarity and digital fluency [2, 4].

## Section Ordering Conventions (general — see override above)

- **Strategic default:** Header → Professional Summary → Core Competencies/Skills (Categorized) →
  Professional Experience → Certifications → Education [5-8].
- **Experience-first alternative:** Summary → Professional Experience → Personal Projects → Skills →
  Education — often preferred for technical senior roles so skills are evaluated in context of recent
  achievements rather than in isolation [9-11]. **This is the pattern this project already follows.**
- **Executive search standard:** for VP/C-suite roles, a hybrid format places a "Selected Achievements"
  or "Strategic Leadership Highlights" section immediately below the summary, above the chronological
  work history, to signal scale and outcome within the first five seconds [12-14].

## Skills-Block Design: Categorized vs. Run-on

A "skills cloud" or single run-on comma paragraph is an outdated pattern that triggers spam filters or
causes parsing errors [11, 15].

- **Categorized grid rule.** Use 2-4 bold-labeled categorized groups (e.g., *Methodologies*, *Digital
  Ecosystems*, *Governance*). Bold category label on its own line, comma-separated list of 8-12 total
  skills on the next line [10, 11, 16].
- **The "T-shaped" model.** Aim for a 70/30 split between technical/domain skills and
  leadership/governance skills [17].
- **Omit ratings.** No graphical star ratings, progress bars, or percentages — often invisible to
  parsers and subjectively meaningless to recruiters [18-20]. Use plain-text proficiency labels
  (*Expert*, *Advanced*) if needed [18].

## Bold Usage Rules

Over-bolding confuses parsers and degrades human readability. Enforce selective bolding:

- **Standard bold targets:** candidate name, section headings, employer names, job titles, skills
  category labels, education institutions, additional-information labels (e.g., **Languages:**) [10, 11].
- **The "no-body" rule:** never bold entire sentences or bullet-point bodies — keeps the reader's eye on
  structural anchors, not random emphasis [10, 11].

## Sub-Group and Achievement Formatting

- **"Key Deliveries" standard.** Standalone italic line (*Key Deliveries*), sitting below the employer
  and role title in the visual hierarchy [11].
- **Density caps.** One sub-group per role, only when there are two or more major achievements. A
  single achievement stays a normal bullet [11].
- **Bullet formula.** [Strong action verb] + [scope/tool] + [quantified outcome] (e.g., "Led
  3-engineer migration off legacy MongoDB to Postgres, reducing read latency by 75%") [21, 22].

## Scannability and Reading Patterns

Scannability in 2026 is designed for "the 7-second problem" [3, 23]:

- **Top-third rule.** Name, professional identity, and 3-4 high-signal keywords must be visible in the
  top third of page one to pass the initial human skim [24].
- **F-pattern optimization.** Readers scan horizontally across the top, then vertically down the left
  side. Place job titles/dates on the left or use clear bold anchors so the eye catches the seniority
  progression [25, 26].
- **White space strategy.** Avoid walls of text — bullets strictly 1-2 lines (3 max), 6-8pt spacing
  between paragraphs [27-29].
- **File consistency.** Single-column layout. Two-column layouts cause a "reading-order scramble" where
  parsers interleave sidebar text into the work history [30, 31].

## Alignment with this project's rules

Confirms and grounds `rules/cv-authoring.md`'s existing formatting rules: 2-4 categorized skills groups
with 8-12 skills, no run-on skills paragraph, bold-only targets list, "Key Deliveries" as the standalone
italic sub-group label, and the bullet-density caps. The one place general 2026 convention diverges from
this project is section order (skills-before-experience) — already overridden above per the
council-validated 2026-06-11 decision.
