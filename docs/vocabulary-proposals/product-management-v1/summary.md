## The question the owner added: is "product owner" the same job as "product manager"?

**Yes. One occupation, two names, two emphases.** Three independent lines agree:

1. **The adverts.** Among the 285 in-family adverts, every floor item below is asked for by at
   least 63% of the adverts titled Product Owner AND at least 64% of those titled Product Manager.
   The difference is emphasis, not work. PO-titled adverts lean team-facing: backlog 90% vs 71%,
   user stories 83% vs 64%, agile skill 63% vs 22%, UAT 42% vs 18%. PM-titled adverts lean
   market-facing: roadmap 84% vs 73%, metrics 80% vs 66%, discovery 75% vs 63%, P&L 33% vs 17%.
2. **The authorities** (`po-vs-pm-web-research.md`). The Scrum Guide calls Product Owner an
   accountability, not a job title. SVPG, Pragmatic Institute and Scrum.org call PO work a subset
   of product management. SAFe splits the two on purpose but defines them as one job done by two
   people. No official occupation list (O*NET, ANZSCO, SSG) names Product Owner as an occupation.
3. **The market's own wording.** One HK bank advert titled "Digital Product Manager" asks the
   person to "act as Digital Product Owner". LinkedIn returns 164 of the same adverts for both
   searches.

**This is why it is a family and not the rejected widening** (2026-08-20). The floor questions sit
at the centre of a product owner's work, not at its edge.

**Hong Kong vs Australia.** It is the same job in both markets. Hong Kong differs in two ways:

- **"Product Manager" is a polluted title in HK.** 32 of 117 HK PM-titled adverts (27%) manage a
  bank or insurance product commercially (rates, fees, coverage, campaigns), and 9 more are
  marketing. In AU the figures are 27 of 165 (16%) and 3.
- **HK product owners are narrower.** Among the 14 HK PO-titled in-family adverts, roadmap and
  metrics each appear in only 43%. They are backlog-and-stakeholder jobs inside banks. This is
  thin (14 adverts) and is not a voting market split.

## What this family covers, and where its edge is

It covers deciding what a digital or technology product does and why, and steering the team that
builds it:
- owning the product vision and roadmap;
- understanding user and market needs;
- setting priorities, and owning the backlog the build team works from;
- answering for what the product achieves.

Both market names are inside, including their technical, digital, platform and data variants.

Outside it:
- the commercial bank or insurance "product manager", including a PM title that names a financial
  product line (mortgages, cards, loans, policies);
- product marketing;
- running delivery (IT Project Manager);
- specifying requirements without owning priorities (Business Analyst).

The label is **"Product Manager"**: the provider's market uses it 71:20 over "product owner".
"product owner" is a published market search title in all four markets, so it is findable either
way.

**Seven items cleared. The draft floor takes the four in bold**, the cut the owner picks:

1. Have you defined a product's vision or roadmap, meaning what it should become and in what
   order?
2. Have you owned a product backlog, deciding what the build team works on next?
3. Have you researched users or the market to decide what a product should do, for example
   through interviews, competitor analysis or testing ideas?
4. Have you set or tracked a product's success measures, and used the data to decide what to
   change?

**Cleared but cut** (they are first candidates for v2):
- works with the engineering team (86%);
- aligns stakeholders (75%);
- writes stories / requirements (69%).

**Why cut.** They are real, but the neighbouring families' people say yes to them too. "Writes
user stories" is the Business Analyst floor's own item, and stakeholders and team-working are
everyday IT Project Manager work. A must-have that every neighbour also passes does not show the
CV is a product person's.

Not on the list, but recurring in the readers' notes:
- vendor and partner management (~15% of adverts);
- managing or coaching other product managers;
- incident and support ownership after launch;
- PMs prototyping with AI tools.
